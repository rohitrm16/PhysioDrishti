# Knee Rehabilitation Basics

## Common Knee Conditions We Treat

- **Patellofemoral pain syndrome (PFPS)** — anterior knee pain around or behind the kneecap, common in runners and young adults
- **Medial collateral ligament (MCL) sprain** — inner-knee pain from valgus stress, usually sporting
- **ACL post-operative rehab** — structured return-to-sport after anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction
- **Meniscus tear** — medial or lateral joint-line pain, often with locking or giving way
- **Osteoarthritis (OA)** — degenerative joint pain, stiffness after rest, crepitus
- **Iliotibial band syndrome (ITBS)** — lateral knee pain in runners, cyclists
- **Patellar tendinopathy** — infrapatellar pain worsened by loading (stairs, jumping)

## Phase 1 — Acute (Days 0–7)

Goals: reduce swelling, restore range of motion (ROM), prevent muscle inhibition.

- RICE protocol: rest, ice (15–20 min every 2 hours), compression bandage, elevation
- Ankle pumps and static quadriceps contractions to maintain circulation
- Passive ROM: gentle heel slides, gravity-assisted flexion
- Target: achieve 90° flexion before progressing

## Phase 2 — Subacute (Weeks 2–6)

Goals: restore full ROM, early strength, normalise gait.

- Terminal knee extensions (TKE) with resistance band
- Mini squats (0–45°), progressing to 60° as tolerated
- Straight leg raises — 3 sets × 15 reps
- Step-ups (5 cm, 10 cm, 15 cm block progression)
- Cycling (low resistance, seat height adjusted for comfort)
- Proprioception: single-leg balance 30 seconds, eyes open → closed

## Phase 3 — Strengthening (Weeks 6–12)

Goals: symmetrical strength, return to functional activities.

- Full squat (0–90°), wall sits, leg press
- Romanian deadlift for hamstring/glute loading
- Lateral band walks, clamshells for hip abductor support
- Eccentric quad loading (decline squats for patellar tendinopathy)
- Target: Limb Symmetry Index (LSI) ≥ 85% on single-leg hop test

## Phase 4 — Return to Sport / Function

Goals: sport-specific loading, psychological readiness.

- Running progression: walk–jog intervals on flat → gradient → agility
- Hop tests: single-leg hop, triple hop, crossover hop
- Criteria to discharge: LSI ≥ 90%, full painless ROM, no effusion

## Patient Education Points

- Knee pain does not always mean damage — graded loading is safe and therapeutic
- Strengthening the quadriceps, hamstrings, and hip abductors protects the joint
- Avoid prolonged rest — deconditioning slows recovery

## Red Flags for Knee

- Severe effusion appearing within 2 hours of injury (haemarthrosis — refer immediately)
- Locked knee that cannot be passively extended
- Neurovascular compromise: cold, pale foot, absent dorsalis pedis pulse
- Suspected fracture: inability to weight-bear, bony tenderness
