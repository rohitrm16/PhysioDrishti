# Back Pain Red Flags

Physiotherapists are trained to screen for serious spinal pathology before starting treatment. The following symptoms require immediate medical referral — do not proceed with physiotherapy alone.

## Red Flag Symptoms

**Seek emergency care immediately if the patient reports:**

- Sudden loss of bladder or bowel control (may indicate cauda equina syndrome)
- Saddle anaesthesia — numbness in the inner thighs, perineum, or genitals
- Progressive bilateral leg weakness or bilateral leg numbness

**Refer to a doctor promptly if the patient reports:**

- Unexplained weight loss of more than 5 kg in the past 3 months
- Night sweats combined with back pain that does not improve with rest
- Back pain that is constant, progressive, and unrelated to movement
- History of cancer anywhere in the body — back pain may indicate metastasis
- Recent serious infection (e.g., TB, IV drug use, immunosuppression)
- Fever above 38°C combined with back pain
- Age of onset under 20 or over 55 with no prior history of back pain
- Violent trauma such as a road accident or fall from height
- Prolonged corticosteroid use (risk of vertebral fracture)

## What is Not a Red Flag

Most back pain is non-specific and responds well to physiotherapy:

- Localised lumbar ache made worse by movement
- Stiffness first thing in the morning that eases with gentle movement
- Pain that radiates to the buttock or upper thigh
- Pain that varies with posture or activity

## Assessment Notes

Always complete a full subjective assessment (24-hour behaviour, aggravating/easing factors, symptom onset) before classifying back pain. Document red flag screening in every patient file.
